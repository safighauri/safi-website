PVARA Licensing After 5 September: Five Mistakes Foreign Exchanges Are Making in Pakistan

By Safi Ghauri, Managing Partner, Esquare Legal · 20 November 2026

Pakistan's virtual asset regime is no longer a policy discussion. The Virtual Assets Act, 2026 is in force, and on 21 August 2026 PVARA notified the Pakistan Virtual Asset Services Regulations, 2026 (S.R.O. 1419(I)/2026). The transitional deadline under section 70 of the Act, 5 September 2026, has passed. Anyone who was already serving Pakistan and did not file for an NOC by then is now operating outside the perimeter.

I currently act for one of the world's largest crypto exchanges and several fintechs before PVARA. The same five mistakes keep coming up in the files I see from foreign operators.

1. Assuming a VARA, MAS or MiCA licence carries over

It does not. Neither the Regulations nor PVARA's published FAQ provide for recognition of foreign licences. A Dubai or EU licence is useful evidence of track record in the business plan and fit-and-proper material, but the Pakistani application is assessed in full, on its own terms.

2. Thinking "we don't have a Pakistani entity, so we're not caught"

Regulation 3(4) treats an activity as carried on in Pakistan where it targets, solicits, promotes to or onboards persons in Pakistan, including through PKR rails or Pakistan-targeted marketing. Being merely accessible from Pakistan is not enough on its own (Reg 3(5)), but only if you genuinely do not market there, do not onboard Pakistani users and take reasonable steps to block them. Wilfully providing an unlicensed service carries up to five years' imprisonment, a fine of up to PKR 50 million, or both (section 54(1)).

3. Filing the NOC before planning the Pakistani build-out

The NOC (Reg 6) is filed before the company exists. But the licence application (Reg 7) must follow incorporation and FMU registration within the NOC's validity: three months, extendable once by up to three. Directors, the resident key individual, the Pakistani AML/CFT manual, the custody model and the jurisdictional delivery model all have to be planned from day one, because Form I asks for them.

4. Under-reading the local presence and capital rules

The licensee must be a Companies Act, 2017 company with at least three directors, a registered office in Pakistan and at least one key individual resident in Pakistan with real decision-making authority (Reg 10(4)). Foreign ownership of up to 100% is allowed. Minimum paid-up capital runs from PKR 15 million for Advisory Services to PKR 500 million for Exchange, Lending and Borrowing, Derivatives and Mining Related Services, and it must be maintained at all times.

5. Treating the statutory clocks as fixed

PVARA decides a complete NOC within 60 days and a complete licence application within 90 days (extendable by 60 for complex files). The word that matters is "complete": both clocks run only once PVARA confirms completeness in writing, and they stop while PVARA waits for information. In practice, elapsed time is driven by how fast your file is made complete, not by the statutory period.

What to do now

Map your business to the eleven licence categories, check the capital floor for each, and work backwards from the NOC's three-month validity to plan incorporation and the licence filing. Products that do not fit a category belong in the Regulatory Sandbox, which can lead directly to a licence without a separate NOC (Reg 7(6)). It is not a shortcut for an exchange or custody business that fits an existing category.
